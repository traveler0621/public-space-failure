  into restrained liminal images by preserving the camera, local identity,

  cleanup, emptiness, or film texture alone do not count.

# Public Space Failure

Preserve the camera.

Preserve the identity of the place.

Do not preserve spatial geometry.

Spatial geometry is the main editable material.

Visual language: 2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism.

This visual language should remain consistent across outputs.

The result should never look like clean contemporary digital photography.

Visual treatment comes last.

If only the filter changes, the task has failed.

---

## 1. Lock the Source Identity

Preserve:

- original camera position
- camera height
- framing
- lens feeling
- perspective logic
- horizon / vanishing direction
- local architectural materials
- recognizable signage when important
- public-space or transit identity
- real wear, texture, and documentary realism

The final image should still feel like the same real place.

Do NOT interpret this as “leave the architecture unchanged.”

The camera and identity are locked.

The spatial geometry is not.

---

## 2. Read the Spatial Promise

Identify what the space normally promises.

Examples:

- door → another place
- corridor → passage
- staircase → another level
- escalator → arrival
- platform → departure
- exit → outside
- route → progress
- waiting area → an event

Then break ONE of those promises.

The failure should grow from the existing architecture.

Do not add random surreal objects.

---

## 3. Spatial Geometry MUST Change

Every output must contain one clearly visible architectural or circulation failure.

Color grading, grain, lighting, atmosphere, emptiness, cleanup, or removing people alone do NOT count.

Allowed changes include:

- extend
- enlarge
- repeat
- loop
- sink
- layer
- fold
- overlap
- enclose
- compress
- misalign
- redirect
- duplicate
- displace
- reclassify inside / outside

At least one of these must visibly change:

- spatial depth
- scale
- route continuity
- destination
- threshold behavior
- vertical movement
- interior / exterior boundary
- floor or platform layering
- architectural repetition
- public-space function

If the final result could be mistaken for the source image, the transformation is too weak.

If only the filter changed, the task has failed.

---

## 4. Choose ONE Failure

Use one dominant spatial failure only.

### Door → Larger Space

A narrow or ordinary doorway reveals a full-scale public space too large to physically fit behind it.

### Exit → Interior

An exit leads into another interior instead of outside.

### Infinite Continuation

A corridor, platform, passage, tunnel, or column sequence continues beyond plausible depth.

### Route → Loop

Movement continues but quietly returns to itself.

### Destination Failure

The architecture still directs movement, but arrival disappears.

### Descent Failure

A staircase or lower route continues into a larger, deeper, or repeated lower public level.

### Layer Failure

Another version of the same platform, floor, corridor, or room appears above, below, behind, or within the original.

### Interior / Exterior Failure

Outside becomes enclosed, or an exit never fully becomes outside.

### Recursive Public Space

A public space contains another version of itself.

Examples:

- station inside station
- platform beneath platform
- corridor behind corridor
- full-scale passage inside a narrow door

### Compression / Collapse

A large public environment reduces to a few recognizable fragments of its own identity.

The failure must be clearly visible.

---

## 5. Match the Failure to the Image

### Door / threshold

Prefer:

- full-scale corridor behind a narrow door
- larger hidden interior
- exit becoming deeper interior

### Corridor

Prefer:

- impossible extension
- loop
- repetition
- unresolved destination

### Staircase

Prefer:

- clear descent failure
- larger lower public space
- repeated lower levels
- landing opening into an impossible volume

Do not merely hide steps.

### Escalator

Prefer:

- withheld arrival
- continued ascent beyond the destination
- escalator entering an impossible upper volume

### Platform / waiting area

Prefer:

- hidden lower platform
- repeated platform
- route loop
- destination failure
- public function failure

### Monitor / reflection

Prefer:

- another spatial layer
- displaced architecture
- reflection behaving physically

### Indoor / outdoor transition

Prefer:

- outside absorbed into interior
- courtyard becoming enclosed
- exit failing to become outside

---

## 6. Preserve Only Key Anchors

Keep only the minimum anchors needed to preserve identity.

Usually retain 2–4:

- station name
- platform edge
- tiled wall
- staircase
- escalator
- train
- bench
- door
- clock
- monitor
- column rhythm
- one person if useful for scale

Do not preserve every real-world detail.

The anchors preserve the place.

The surrounding geometry is allowed to fail.

---

## 7. Simplify

Remove or reduce when unnecessary:

- extra people
- advertisements
- redundant signage
- background clutter
- secondary furniture
- temporary signs
- technical distractions
- visual noise
- phone / interface overlays

The final image should usually feel:

- quieter
- emptier
- more distilled
- more spatially legible

Do not preserve clutter out of habit.

Empty space is part of the method.

---

## 8. Strength Test

Before rendering, ask:

Would the spatial failure still be obvious in black and white?

If no:

strengthen it.

Would the result still look almost identical to the source?

If yes:

strengthen it.

The architectural change should be readable at thumbnail size.

Do not continue to final image treatment until the spatial failure is clearly visible.

---

## 9. Liminal, Not Horror

Preferred mood:

- quiet
- suspended
- empty
- slightly uncanny
- emotionally cool
- calm but spatially wrong
- observational

Avoid:

- monsters
- gore
- horror faces
- haunted-house imagery
- supernatural threats
- theatrical darkness
- jump scares
- extreme horror grading

Use Backrooms as spatial logic, not as a visual template.

Do not automatically generate yellow office mazes.

Discover the Backrooms already latent inside the real place.

---

## 10. Visual Treatment LAST

Only after the spatial edit is clearly complete, apply:

**2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism**

The image should feel like a real early-2000s European documentary, travel photograph, or public-space observational frame captured with older digital, compact-camera, or DV-era equipment.

Preferred qualities:

- warm yellow to slightly yellow-green fluorescent cast
- institutional public-space lighting
- slightly sickly but controlled fluorescent tone
- dirty off-whites
- pale beige
- muted olive-green undertones
- tired fluorescent yellow
- low to moderate contrast
- muted colors
- slight optical softness
- gentle soft focus
- subtle light bloom / halation
- fine visible grain
- slightly faded blacks
- mild haze
- modest dynamic range
- imperfect white balance
- ordinary exposure
- believable shadow detail
- worn architectural texture
- unpolished documentary realism

The yellow-green cast should feel caused by:

- real fluorescent public-space lighting
- imperfect white balance
- older digital camera response
- institutional interior illumination

It must not look like a simple global yellow filter.

The image should feel observational, slightly degraded, and believable.

The impossible space should feel accidentally documented.

---

## 11. Exterior Daylight Rule

If the source image is outdoors, do not force the entire image into artificial yellow-green lighting.

Preserve believable daylight.

Still retain:

- subdued documentary color
- modest contrast
- slight grain
- gentle softness
- imperfect camera response
- mildly aged image texture

If sheltered fluorescent areas exist, the yellow-green cast may appear locally.

Color should remain physically believable.

---

## 12. Avoid Modern Digital Polish

Avoid:

- HDR
- hyper-sharp digital detail
- glossy architectural visualization
- polished CGI
- luxury editorial grading
- oversaturated color
- teal-and-orange grading
- blockbuster lighting
- strong cinematic rim light
- pristine blacks
- excessive clarity
- obvious AI sharpness
- commercial photography finish

If the final image looks clean, neutral, modern, or digitally polished, restore:

- softer contrast
- fine grain
- optical softness
- faded blacks
- imperfect white balance
- fluorescent documentary texture

---

## 13. Execution Order

Always work in this order:

1. Read the source spatial promise.
2. Select 2–4 key anchors.
3. Choose one spatial failure.
4. Change the spatial geometry clearly.
5. Verify the failure works without color grading.
6. Simplify clutter and unnecessary people.
7. Preserve local identity.
8. Apply the visual treatment LAST.

Do not begin with the filter.

Spatial structure comes first.

Image treatment comes last.

---

## 14. Dry Run Mode

If the user asks for a dry run:

Do not generate or edit an image.

Treat requests for **Spatial Critic Mode**, concept analysis, or failure analysis as Dry Run Mode.

Output only:

1. Source spatial promise
2. 2–4 key spatial anchors
3. The single spatial failure
4. Which architectural geometry visibly changes
5. How obvious the transformation would be
6. What clutter / people / signage is removed
7. What identity anchors remain
8. Final 2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism treatment
9. PASS or FAIL

PASS only if the architectural change would remain obvious without color grading.

---

## Final Rule

Preserve the camera.

Preserve the place.

Transform the space.

One failure.

Few anchors.

Less clutter.

Filter last.

The spatial failure must survive without the filter.

The visual language must remain consistent across outputs.

If only the image treatment changed, the task failed.

If the place remains recognizable but its spatial promise has clearly broken, the task succeeded.
