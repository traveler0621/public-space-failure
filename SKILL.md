# Public Space Failure

Preserve the camera.

Preserve the place.

Transform the space.

Visual language: 2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism.

Visual treatment never substitutes for spatial transformation.

The final image should feel like the same real public place, but one spatial
promise has visibly failed.

The space may become impossible.

The photograph should still feel true.

---

## 1. Core Principle

Public Space Failure is about spatial failure first.

The source photograph should remain recognizable as the same real location, but
one architectural, circulatory, functional, or threshold rule must visibly stop
working.

The transformation should feel:
- restrained
- believable
- quiet
- spatially clear
- non-horror
- documentary

Do not create fantasy architecture for its own sake.

Do not treat color grading, atmosphere, emptiness, or cleanup as the main
transformation.

---

## 2. Camera & Identity Lock

Preserve:

- original camera position
- camera height
- framing
- lens feeling
- perspective logic
- horizon
- vanishing structure
- local architectural materials
- recognizable place identity
- important signage when needed
- transit or public-space identity
- documentary realism
- real wear and texture

The viewer should still recognize the source location.

Important:

Do NOT interpret preservation as “leave the architecture unchanged.”

The camera and identity are locked.

The spatial geometry is not.

---

## 3. Spatial Geometry MUST Change

Every output MUST visibly alter the spatial structure of the source image.

Color grading, film grain, lighting changes, fluorescent yellow-green tone,
cleanup, emptiness, crowd removal, or atmosphere alone do NOT count.

At least one major spatial relationship must clearly change.

Allowed transformations include:

- extend
- enlarge
- repeat
- loop
- sink
- fold
- overlap
- enclose
- compress
- misalign
- layer
- duplicate
- redirect
- displace
- reclassify inside and outside

The transformation must affect one or more of:

- spatial depth
- scale
- route continuity
- destination
- threshold behavior
- vertical movement
- interior / exterior boundary
- floor or platform layering
- architectural repetition
- public-space function

If the output could easily be mistaken for the original photograph, the
transformation is too weak.

If only the filter changed, the task has failed.

---

## 4. Failure Test

Before finalizing, ask:

If all color grading, grain, fluorescent atmosphere, and image treatment were
removed, would the image still contain a clearly impossible public-space
condition?

If no, the transformation is insufficient.

Strengthen the spatial geometry before continuing.

The spatial failure should remain readable in black and white.

---

## 5. Read the Spatial Promise

Before editing, identify what the space normally promises.

Examples:

- corridor → passage
- staircase → another level
- escalator → arrival
- platform → departure
- waiting area → an event
- door → another place
- exit → outside
- route → progress
- railway → movement between destinations

Then break one of those promises.

The failure should grow from the existing architecture.

Do not add random surreal objects.

---

## 6. Choose ONE Spatial Failure

Use one dominant spatial failure only.

Do not combine several unrelated surreal ideas.

The result should be easy to describe in one sentence.

Examples:

- a small door opens into a much larger public interior
- an exit leads deeper inside instead of outdoors
- a platform continues into itself
- a corridor extends without arrival
- a staircase descends into an impossible lower public level
- an escalator rises toward a destination that never resolves
- a second platform exists beneath the track bed
- an exterior courtyard becomes part of a larger interior
- a monitor behaves like another spatial layer
- a public room collapses into fragments of its own identity

The failure must be visibly clear.

---

## 7. Public Space Failure Logic

Use these as the main concept families.

### Door → Larger Space

A modest doorway or threshold reveals a public interior too large to physically
fit behind it.

### Exit → Interior

An exit appears to lead outside, but outside never truly begins.

### Infinite Continuation

A corridor, platform, tunnel, passage, or column sequence continues far beyond
reasonable reality.

### Route → Loop

Movement remains possible, but progress disappears.

The route continues while quietly returning to itself.

### Destination Failure

Architecture still directs movement, but arrival no longer resolves.

### Public Function Failure

A platform, waiting area, corridor, foyer, or station remains visually usable
but loses its actual purpose.

### Layer Failure

Another displaced or incomplete version of the same public space appears above,
below, behind, or within the original.

### Descent Failure

A staircase, lower route, or platform continues into a larger, deeper, repeated,
or physically impossible lower public level.

### Interior / Exterior Failure

The boundary between inside and outside quietly stops functioning.

### Screen / Reflection Failure

A monitor, surveillance image, window, or reflection begins behaving like
another spatial layer rather than a flat image.

### Compression / Collapse

A large public environment collapses into a few recognizable identity fragments
such as:

- station sign
- bench
- clock
- monitor
- train fragment
- tiled wall
- column
- waiting furniture

### Recursive Public Space

A public space contains another version of itself.

Examples:
- a station inside a station
- a platform beneath a platform
- a corridor opening into another corridor of the same type
- a public room nested inside itself

---

## 8. Choose Failure by Image Structure

### Door or threshold

Prefer:
- larger hidden interior
- impossible room behind ordinary opening
- exit becoming deeper interior
- threshold connecting spaces that should not physically fit together

### Corridor or passage

Prefer:
- extension
- looping
- repetition
- unresolved destination
- enlargement into a quieter public void

### Staircase

Prefer:
- clear descent failure
- larger lower public space
- repeated lower levels
- landing opening into an impossible volume
- nested descent

Do not solve staircase images with tiny edits.

Do not merely hide steps.

The lower spatial transformation must be clearly visible.

### Escalator

Prefer:
- withheld arrival
- enlarged upper volume
- vertical circulation without resolution
- escalator entering a larger impossible chamber

### Platform or waiting area

Prefer:
- destination failure
- loop
- layered platform
- repeated platform
- public function failure
- hidden lower platform
- stronger emptiness

### Monitor, reflection, or surveillance view

Prefer:
- another spatial layer
- displaced architecture
- reflection behaving physically
- screen becoming evidence of deeper space

### Indoor / outdoor transition

Prefer:
- outside absorbed into interior
- courtyard becoming enclosed
- exit failing to become outside
- daylight architecture continuing into a fluorescent public interior

### Large hall or open public room

Prefer:
- excessive empty expansion
- repeated lower or adjacent layers
- scale mismatch
- functionally intact but existentially empty waiting space

---

## 9. Key Spatial Anchors

Preserve only the minimum key spatial anchors needed to keep the place
recognizable and believable.

Usually retain 2–4 anchors.

Typical anchors:

- station name
- line sign
- distinctive tiled wall
- column system
- platform edge
- railway track
- bench
- staircase
- escalator
- train
- door
- clock
- monitor
- corridor opening
- one person if useful for scale

The anchors preserve identity.

The surrounding geometry is allowed to fail.

---

## 10. Simplification Priority

Simplify before adding detail.

Remove or reduce when unnecessary:

- extra people
- redundant advertisements
- secondary signage
- background clutter
- unnecessary furniture
- random technical objects
- distracting posters
- visual noise
- interface overlays
- phone UI artifacts

Do not preserve clutter out of habit.

Do not preserve every sign, passenger, advertisement, or object just because it
appears in the source.

If too many real-world details weaken the spatial failure, remove them.

The final image should usually feel:

- quieter
- emptier
- more legible
- more spatially exposed
- more distilled than the source

Empty space is part of the method.

---

## 11. People

People are optional.

Keep a person only when they help:

- establish scale
- express waiting
- show movement
- strengthen documentary realism
- clarify the relationship between body and architecture

Otherwise reduce or remove them.

If retained:

- keep ordinary behavior
- preserve realistic scale
- avoid theatrical posing
- avoid fantasy styling
- avoid horror characterization

A single person may be useful as a witness.

A crowd usually weakens the spatial failure.

---

## 12. Transformation Strength Rule

The result must show an unmistakable architectural or spatial change.

If the image would still look almost identical to the source after removing the
color treatment, the transformation is too weak.

At least one major spatial condition must visibly fail.

Examples:

- a door opens into a larger impossible interior
- a platform contains another unreachable platform below it
- a corridor extends beyond the plausible depth of the building
- circulation loops back impossibly
- stairs descend into a much larger lower space
- an exterior area becomes enclosed
- a track bed contains another public-space layer
- a station repeats beneath itself

The spatial failure should be noticeable even at thumbnail size.

---

## 13. Minimal Means, Strong Geometry

The concept should be strong, but the visual intervention should remain
restrained.

Prefer changing existing relationships through:

- extension
- repetition
- looping
- sinking
- layering
- overlapping
- enclosing
- compression
- displacement
- removal
- misalignment

Avoid:

- random surreal props
- giant sculptural objects
- unrelated portals
- decorative fantasy elements
- theatrical architecture
- concept-art spectacle

The best result should feel as if the original photograph already contained the
possibility of failure.

---

## 14. No Architectural Overdesign

Do not redesign the location into an unrelated new building.

Avoid adding unrelated:

- giant monumental walls
- sculptural installations
- fantasy ceilings
- sci-fi lighting systems
- decorative portals
- spectacular geometry with no source connection

However:

Architectural geometry MAY change substantially when required to create the
spatial failure.

Preserve the architectural language.

Do not preserve the original spatial logic.

---

## 15. Liminal, Not Horror

The mood should feel:

- quiet
- suspended
- empty
- slightly uncanny
- emotionally cool
- underused
- calm but spatially wrong
- observational

Avoid:

- monsters
- blood
- gore
- horror faces
- threatening shadows
- jump scares
- haunted-house imagery
- supernatural threats
- extreme darkness
- theatrical fear

The goal is spatial uncertainty, not terror.

---

## 16. Backrooms Logic, Not Backrooms Style

Use Backrooms as spatial logic only.

Useful qualities:

- impossible extension
- looping circulation
- oversized interiors
- repeated infrastructure
- failed thresholds
- unresolved destination
- suspended public function
- excessive emptiness

Do not automatically generate:

- yellow office mazes
- horror corridors
- monsters
- fog
- creepypasta visuals

Discover the Backrooms already latent inside the real place.

---

## 17. Visual Treatment LAST

Only after the spatial transformation is clearly complete, apply:

**2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism**

The image should feel like a real early-2000s European documentary, travel
photograph, or public-space observational frame captured with older digital,
compact-camera, or DV-era equipment.

Preferred qualities:

- warm yellow to slightly yellow-green fluorescent cast
- institutional public-space lighting
- slightly sickly but controlled fluorescent tone
- dirty off-whites
- pale beige
- muted olive-green undertones
- tired fluorescent yellow
- low to moderate contrast
- muted colors
- slight optical softness
- gentle soft focus
- subtle light bloom or halation
- visible fine grain
- slightly faded blacks
- mild haze
- modest dynamic range
- imperfect white balance
- ordinary exposure
- believable shadow detail
- worn architectural texture
- unpolished documentary realism

The image should feel observational, slightly degraded, and believable.

The impossible space should feel accidentally documented.

---

## 18. Fluorescent Color Rule

The yellow-green cast should feel caused by:

- real fluorescent public-space lighting
- imperfect white balance
- older digital camera response
- institutional interior illumination

It should not look like a simple global yellow filter.

Do not turn every white surface bright yellow.

Keep tonal variation.

Preferred color behavior:

- warm dirty whites
- pale yellow-beige highlights
- subtle olive or green contamination
- slightly tired blacks
- restrained saturation
- low-key fluorescent glow

---

## 19. Exterior Daylight

If the source image is outdoors, do not force the entire image into artificial
yellow-green lighting.

Preserve believable daylight.

Still retain:

- subdued documentary color
- modest contrast
- slight grain
- gentle softness
- imperfect camera response
- mildly aged texture

If sheltered fluorescent areas exist, the yellow-green cast may appear locally.

Color must remain physically believable.

---

## 20. Avoid Modern Digital Polish

Avoid:

- HDR
- hyper-sharp digital detail
- glossy architectural visualization
- polished CGI
- luxury editorial grading
- oversaturated color
- teal-and-orange grading
- blockbuster lighting
- strong cinematic rim light
- pristine blacks
- excessive clarity
- obvious AI sharpness
- commercial photography finish

If the final image looks clean, neutral, modern, or digitally polished, restore:

- softer contrast
- fine grain
- optical softness
- faded blacks
- imperfect white balance
- fluorescent documentary texture

---

## 21. Execution Order

Always work in this order:

1. Read the source architecture.
2. Identify the source spatial promise.
3. Select 2–4 key spatial anchors.
4. Choose one dominant spatial failure.
5. Alter the spatial geometry clearly.
6. Verify the spatial failure works without color grading.
7. Simplify clutter, people, and redundant visual information.
8. Preserve local identity and documentary realism.
9. Apply the 2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism treatment LAST.

Do not begin with color grading.

Do not treat the filter as the transformation.

Spatial structure comes first.

Image treatment comes last.

---

## 22. Final Quality Check

Before finishing, verify:

- Is the source location still recognizable?
- Has the spatial geometry clearly changed?
- Would the failure remain visible without the filter?
- Is there one dominant spatial failure?
- Is the transformation strong enough to notice immediately?
- Does the failure grow from the source architecture?
- Are only the key spatial anchors preserved?
- Has unnecessary clutter been reduced?
- Is the image quieter and more distilled than the source?
- Are people kept only when useful?
- Is the mood liminal but non-horror?
- Does the image feel like 2000s documentary fluorescent realism?
- Does it avoid modern glossy digital polish?
- Was the visual treatment applied only after the spatial edit?

If only the filter changed:
the task has failed.

If the image still looks almost identical to the source:
the task has failed.

If the place remains recognizable but its spatial promise has clearly broken:
the task has succeeded.

---

## 23. Dry Run Mode

If the user asks for a dry run, do not generate or edit an image.

Output only:

1. Source spatial promise
2. 1–3 or 2–4 key spatial anchors
3. The single spatial failure
4. Which architectural geometry would visibly change
5. What people, clutter, signage, or distractions would be removed
6. What must remain unchanged
7. The final 2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism treatment

In dry run mode:

Do not call image-generation tools.

Reject any plan in which the spatial change would remain almost identical to the
source without color grading.

---

## Guiding Lines

Preserve the camera.

Preserve the place.

Do not preserve spatial geometry.

Read the spatial promise.

Break one spatial rule clearly.

Keep only the strongest anchors.

Remove what weakens the idea.

Make the architectural failure visible.

Apply the image treatment last.

Make the space quieter.

Make the failure stronger.

Keep the camera imperfect and honest.

The architecture remains believable.

The logic no longer does.
