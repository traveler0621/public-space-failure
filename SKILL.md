# Public Space Failure

Preserve the camera.

Preserve the identity of the place.

Do not preserve spatial geometry.

Spatial geometry is the primary editable material.

Visual treatment never substitutes for spatial transformation.

The result should still feel like the same real place, but one spatial rule must
visibly stop working.

---

## 1. Camera & Identity Lock

Preserve:

- original camera position
- camera height
- framing
- lens feeling
- perspective logic
- horizon
- major local materials
- recognizable signage when important
- transit or architectural identity
- documentary realism

The viewer should still recognize the source location.

Do NOT interpret preservation as “leave the architecture unchanged.”

---

## 2. Spatial Geometry MUST Change

Every output MUST visibly alter the spatial structure of the source image.

Color grading, grain, lighting, atmosphere, emptiness, cleanup, or removing
people alone do NOT count.

At least one major spatial relationship must change.

Allowed transformations include:

- extend
- enlarge
- repeat
- loop
- sink
- fold
- overlap
- enclose
- compress
- misalign
- layer
- duplicate
- redirect
- reclassify inside and outside

The transformation must affect one or more of:

- depth
- scale
- route continuity
- destination
- threshold behavior
- vertical movement
- interior / exterior boundary
- floor or platform layering
- architectural repetition
- public-space function

If the output could be mistaken for the original photograph, the transformation
is too weak.

If only the filter changed, the task has failed.

---

## 3. Read the Spatial Promise

Before editing, identify what the space normally promises.

Examples:

- corridor → passage
- staircase → another level
- escalator → arrival
- platform → departure
- waiting area → event
- door → another place
- exit → outside
- route → progress

Then break one of those promises.

The failure should grow from the existing architecture.

Do not add random surreal objects.

---

## 4. Choose ONE Spatial Failure

Use one dominant spatial failure only.

Examples:

### Door → Larger Space
A normal doorway reveals a space much larger than could physically fit behind it.

### Exit → Interior
An exit leads into another interior instead of outside.

### Infinite Continuation
A corridor, platform, tunnel, or column sequence continues impossibly far.

### Route → Loop
Movement continues but eventually returns to itself.

### Destination Failure
The architecture still directs movement, but arrival disappears.

### Descent Failure
A staircase or lower route continues into a much larger or deeper impossible
public level.

### Layer Failure
Another version of the same platform, floor, corridor, or public room appears
above, below, behind, or inside the original.

### Interior / Exterior Failure
An exterior space becomes enclosed, or an exit never fully becomes outside.

### Recursive Public Space
A station contains another station.
A corridor contains another corridor.
A platform appears beneath itself.

### Compression / Collapse
A large public space reduces into a few recognizable fragments of its own
identity.

The failure must be visually obvious.

---

## 5. Choose Failure by Image Structure

### Door / threshold
Prefer:
- larger hidden interior
- impossible room behind ordinary opening
- exit becoming deeper interior

### Corridor / passage
Prefer:
- extension
- repetition
- loop
- unresolved destination
- enlargement into a quieter space

### Staircase
Prefer:
- clear descent failure
- larger lower public space
- repeated lower levels
- landing opening into an impossible volume

Do not merely hide steps.

### Escalator
Prefer:
- withheld arrival
- enlarged upper volume
- vertical circulation without resolution

### Platform / waiting area
Prefer:
- loop
- destination failure
- layered platform
- repeated platform
- public function failure

### Monitor / reflection
Prefer:
- another spatial layer
- displaced architecture
- reflection behaving physically

### Indoor / outdoor transition
Prefer:
- outside absorbed into interior
- courtyard becoming enclosed
- exit failing to become outside

---

## 6. Simplify the Scene

After choosing the spatial failure, reduce visual noise.

Remove or reduce when unnecessary:

- people
- advertisements
- redundant signage
- clutter
- secondary furniture
- random technical objects
- distracting background details

Keep only the strongest 1–3 spatial anchors.

Typical anchors:

- station sign
- bench
- staircase
- escalator
- platform edge
- train
- door
- tiled wall
- clock
- monitor
- corridor opening
- column rhythm

The final image should usually feel quieter and emptier than the source.

---

## 7. Strong Transformation Test

Before applying the visual treatment, ask:

Would the spatial failure still be obvious if the image were shown in black and
white?

If no, strengthen the spatial transformation.

Would the output still look almost identical to the source?

If yes, strengthen the spatial transformation.

Do not continue to final rendering until the architectural change is clearly
visible.

---

## 8. Liminal, Not Horror

The mood should feel:

- quiet
- suspended
- empty
- slightly uncanny
- emotionally cool
- calm but spatially wrong
- observational

Avoid:

- monsters
- gore
- horror faces
- haunted-house imagery
- jump scares
- supernatural threats
- extreme darkness
- theatrical fear

The goal is spatial uncertainty, not horror.

---

## 9. Backrooms Logic, Not Backrooms Style

Use Backrooms as spatial logic only.

Useful qualities:

- impossible extension
- looping circulation
- oversized interiors
- repeated infrastructure
- failed thresholds
- unresolved destination
- excessive emptiness

Do not automatically generate:

- yellow office mazes
- horror corridors
- monsters
- fog
- creepypasta imagery

Discover the Backrooms already latent inside the real place.

---

## 10. Visual Treatment LAST

Only after the spatial transformation is complete, apply:

**2000s Documentary Fluorescent Realism**

Preferred qualities:

- early-2000s European documentary feeling
- older digital / compact-camera realism
- warm yellow to slightly yellow-green fluorescent cast
- institutional public-space lighting
- low to moderate contrast
- muted colors
- slight optical softness
- gentle soft focus
- subtle halation
- fine visible grain
- slightly faded blacks
- mild haze
- modest dynamic range
- imperfect white balance
- worn architectural texture
- unpolished documentary realism

The yellow-green cast should feel caused by real public-space lighting and
imperfect camera response.

It must not look like a simple global yellow filter.

For exterior daylight scenes, preserve believable daylight and apply only the
aged documentary texture unless fluorescent sheltered areas are present.

Avoid:

- HDR
- hyper-sharp digital detail
- glossy CGI
- luxury editorial grading
- oversaturated color
- teal-and-orange grading
- blockbuster lighting
- pristine blacks
- obvious AI sharpness
- commercial photography finish

The impossible space should feel accidentally documented.

---

## 11. Final Check

Before finishing, verify:

- Is the source location still recognizable?
- Has the spatial geometry clearly changed?
- Is one dominant spatial failure visible?
- Would the failure still read without the filter?
- Is the image quieter and more distilled?
- Are unnecessary people and clutter reduced?
- Is the mood liminal but non-horror?
- Was the image treatment applied only after the spatial edit?

If only the filter changed:
the task has failed.

If the image is still almost identical to the source:
the task has failed.

If the architecture changed clearly while the place still feels real:
the task has succeeded.

---

## Guiding Lines

Preserve the camera.

Preserve the place.

Transform the space.

Break one spatial rule clearly.

Simplify after choosing the failure.

Apply the image treatment last.

The architecture remains believable.

The logic no longer does.
